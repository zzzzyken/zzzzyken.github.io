Ziyang Zhang

M.S. student in Electrical and Computer Engineering, Vanderbilt University

I am a master’s student in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Vanderbilt University, where I work on medical image analysis with Prof. Yuankai Huo and Prof. He Zhao (University of Liverpool). Before Vanderbilt, I received my B.E. in Electronics and Information Engineering from Huazhong University of Science and Technology, and I spent the summer of 2024 at UC Berkeley as a visiting student.

My research interests center on understanding what models learn from data and how we can make their predictions more reliable and generalizable. Moving forward, I am particularly interested in leveraging large language models and vision-language models for data-centric learning, including identifying dataset biases, guiding data curation, and generating informative training examples. I am also interested in developing methods built upon vision-language foundation models that generalize across tasks and domains, moving beyond conventional task-specific learning. While my current research focuses on medical image analysis, I hope to explore these questions in computer vision and artificial intelligence more broadly.

I am applying to Ph.D. programs for Fall 2027.

Outside of research, I live with two cats: Kudryavka the Russian Blue and Donut the Minuet.

selected publications

  1. Look at What Matters: Learning Lesion Prototypes from Severity Labels for Domain-Generalized Diabetic Retinopathy Grading
    Ziyang Zhang and others
    Manuscript under review, 2026
    Abs

    Deep learning models for diabetic retinopathy (DR) grading often generalize poorly to unseen imaging domains, in part because predictions may exploit correlations specific to source datasets rather than retinal pathology. We address this challenge by constructing grading decisions from lesion evidence learned using only image severity labels. Clinical implications of DR grades provide multiple instance constraints that guide competitive learning of lesion and background prototypes at multiple scales. An additive ordinal scoring model then predicts severity exclusively from individual lesion prototype responses, explicitly connecting learned evidence with grading decisions without a global feature classification pathway. To improve the reliability of this evidence, we introduce teacher guided preservation under image degradations and spatial regularization against persistent coordinate preferences. Across six public DR datasets under a leave one domain out protocol, our method attains a mean quadratic weighted kappa of 0.761, outperforming the strongest competing method by 0.036. Localization and prototype removal analyses show that the learned responses align with retinal lesions and contribute to grading decisions. Experiments across five other prototype architectures and under image degradations further demonstrate the generality and robustness of the approach.

  2. BIBM
    PARAGraph: Pathology-Anatomy-Aware Hierarchical Graph for Diabetic Retinopathy Grading
    Ziyang Zhang, Yuankai Huo, Yalin Zheng, and He Zhao
    In IEEE International Conference on Bioinformatics and Biomedicine (BIBM), 2026
    To appear
    Abs PDF Code

    Diabetic retinopathy (DR) remains a leading cause of vision loss among working-age adults worldwide, making reliable severity grading clinically important. Despite strong performance, most deep models formulate DR grading as image-level classification and do not explicitly model clinically grounded evidence, such as lesion types and spatial relations. In this paper, we propose PARAGraph, a Pathology-Anatomy-Aware Hierarchical Graph framework for DR grading. PARAGraph represents each image as a three-level hierarchical graph with lesion-level nodes, intermediate category and region nodes, and global anatomical and semantic nodes. To incorporate medical priors into nodes, we construct an optic disc-fovea-anchored coordinate frame that provides a scale- and rotation-normalized retinal reference system. Within this frame, lesion nodes are encoded with category, normalized area, and anatomical coordinates. To mitigate noisy lesion segmentation, PARAGraph uses a dual-fusion strategy that introduces global visual context into a graph semantic node and a decision-level prediction branch, improving robustness when lesion evidence is unreliable. Extensive experiments on Messidor-2, APTOS, and DDR show that PARAGraph achieves consistent DR grading performance over state-of-the-art methods. Interpretability and robustness analyses further demonstrate that its predictions are clinically grounded, closely associated with lesion evidence and robust to lesion segmentation noise.
